TERRARIUM SPRITZER - COMBINED BUILD (V1+V2, TWO-JAR SYSTEM)
=============================================================


0. READ THIS FIRST: SAFETY RULES
---------------------------------
- MAINS ISOLATION: nothing in this build should ever touch 120V directly at
  the jar. The wall adapter converts to 12V before anything gets near water.
  Keep the AC side (wall plug -> adapter) physically separated from the wet
  zone.
- GFCI: plug the 12V wall adapter into a GFCI-protected outlet, since it's
  operating near water sources.
- FUSE EVERY PUMP BRANCH: you have 4 waterproof fuse holders - use one per
  pump, inline on the 12V+ wire between the power supply and the relay.
- DRIP LOOPS: any wire or tube that runs down into a wet area should have a
  low point ("drip loop") before it reaches an electrical connection, so
  water runs off the loop instead of wicking into the connector.
- POLARITY CHECK every connection before powering on - reversed polarity on
  the pump can damage it; reversed polarity on the ESP32/relay can damage
  the board.
- NEVER let a pump run dry for extended periods - your specific pump model
  tolerates occasional dry running, but don't rely on that; keep the
  reservoir topped off and check tubing for kinks before each session.
- NO OVER-PRESSURE: your pump is rated 85 PSI max but your nozzles are
  low-flow reptile misting nozzles - this is normal, the pump's pressure
  switch will cycle it, don't restrict the nozzle further or block it.
- FAIL-SAFE DEFAULT: firmware should default relays to OFF on boot/reset,
  and no single sensor or logic fault should be able to hold a pump on
  indefinitely - always have a hard maximum on-time per cycle in code,
  independent of any sensor reading.

2. BUILD ORDER
---------------
Work in this order - each stage is testable before you commit to the next,
so problems get caught small.

STAGE A - LOW-VOLTAGE CONTROL CIRCUIT (breadboard first)

  1. Wire the ESP32 to the relay module on the breadboard: ESP32 3.3V ->
     relay VCC, ESP32 GND -> relay GND, two ESP32 GPIO pins -> relay IN1
     and IN2. AVOID GPIO 0, 2, 12, 15 (boot-strapping pins) - pick two
     general-purpose pins instead (e.g., GPIO 25 and GPIO 26 are safe,
     commonly-broken-out choices on this board).
  2. Power the ESP32 via USB from your computer for this stage - don't
     connect 12V yet.
  3. Flash a basic test sketch that toggles each relay channel independently
     (see section 4 for logic). Confirm both relay LEDs click on/off
     correctly from serial commands or a short timed test before moving on.
  4. TEST WITH THE FAN, NOT THE PUMP. Wire one fan (12V, 0.1A) to relay 1's
     switched output, powered from the 12V supply. Confirm the relay
     reliably switches it. This validates your control logic with zero
     water-damage risk before pumps ever get connected.

STAGE B - 12V PUMP CIRCUIT (per pump, x2)

  For each pump:
  1. Cut two lengths of 18AWG wire (red/black) sized to reach from the
     junction box to the pump's location.
  2. Wire path: 12V PSU (+) -> fuse holder -> relay COM -> relay NO ->
     pump (+). Pump (-) returns directly to PSU (-)/common ground.
  3. Crimp spade connectors where wires meet the pump's terminals (if it
     uses spade tabs) - otherwise solder + heat-shrink or use the wire
     nuts/clamps appropriate to its terminal style.
  4. Double-check polarity with the multimeter before connecting to the
     pump.
  5. Build a drip loop into each wire run just before it reaches the pump -
     the wire should dip down and back up before terminating, so any water
     that runs along it drips off at the low point instead of reaching the
     connection.
  6. Bench-test each pump on its own relay channel, dry-run for a few
     seconds only (per the pump's self-priming spec) to confirm it spins
     and the pressure switch behaves, before plumbing is attached.

STAGE C - PLUMBING

  1. RESERVOIR CAP: drill two holes in the distilled-water jug's cap,
     sized to snugly pass your 1/4" tubing. Thread one length of tubing
     through each hole, leaving several inches of slack inside the jug.
     Seal around each hole with hot glue or silicone once tubing position
     is set - you want a stable dip depth, not a moving tube.
  2. PER LINE: reservoir tubing (1/4") -> barb reducer (1/4" to 3/8") ->
     Timsec filter (3/8" barb) -> pump inlet (3/8" barb, direct
     connection, no adapter needed) -> pump outlet (3/8" barb) -> reducer
     back down to 1/4" if your onward tubing is 1/4" -> tubing run to the
     jar -> nozzle.
  3. Secure every barb connection with a small hose clamp - push-fit alone
     isn't reliable on a barb joint that will see repeated pressure
     cycling from the pump's pressure switch.
  4. Route each tubing run with a drip loop before it reaches any
     electronics, same principle as the wiring.
  5. Mount each nozzle at the jar per your zoning plan (section 3) - moss
     jar nozzle aimed for even coverage, lichen jar nozzle aimed for
     indirect/light coverage away from where the lichen sits.
  6. Fill the reservoir, then run each pump briefly with the nozzle open
     to bleed air from the lines and confirm actual mist output before
     final placement.

STAGE D - ENCLOSURE AND FINAL WIRING

  1. Mount the ESP32 and both relay channels inside the IP67 junction box.
  2. Bring the 12V supply, both fused pump-branch wires, and the two pump
     cable runs in through cable glands or a grommeted pass-through in the
     box wall - not through an open gap.
  3. Route the USB cable out to wherever you're powering the ESP32 (USB
     wall adapter or a computer during development - if this runs
     unattended long-term, you'll want a small USB wall charger, not just
     a laptop).
  4. Close up the box, do a final visual check for pinched wires or
     tubing, and power on.


3. JAR LAYOUT
--------------
MOSS JAR: nozzle positioned for even mist coverage of the jar interior.
Place your Sphagnum (the reddish-maroon moss) lowest and closest to the
nozzle - it's the wettest-preferring specimen you have. Standard
cushion/mat moss sits in the general mist cone.

LICHEN JAR: nozzle aimed for indirect, light coverage - not a direct spray
target. Elevate lichen specimens (especially the pale, thread-like
Usnea-type one) on rock or bark, positioned outside the direct mist cone,
relying on ambient humidity rather than direct wetting. This jar's mist
cycle should run far less often and briefly than the moss jar's.


4. FIRMWARE LOGIC
------------------
Core rules to build into the sketch, not just nice-to-haves:

  - Relay 1 = moss jar pump, Relay 2 = lichen jar pump.
  - HARD INTERLOCK: never allow both relays to be HIGH at the same time.
    This is your fix for the shared 5A power supply only having headroom
    for one 2.5A pump at a time with any real margin. Enforce this in code
    (e.g., a single activePump state variable) rather than just hoping the
    schedules don't overlap.
  - DEFAULT OFF ON BOOT: both relay pins should be explicitly set LOW/OFF
    as the very first thing in setup(), before anything else runs.
  - HARD MAX ON-TIME PER CYCLE: cap each mist burst (e.g., a few seconds)
    in code regardless of what triggers it, so a logic bug can't hold a
    pump on indefinitely.
  - TWO INDEPENDENT SCHEDULES: moss jar cycles frequently (short interval,
    e.g., every few hours), lichen jar cycles rarely (long interval, e.g.,
    once a day or less) - exact numbers are a starting point to tune by
    observation, not something to treat as fixed; watch how quickly each
    jar dries out over the first week and adjust.
  - Start with plain timed cycles (no sensors) - this matches where your
    project already is. Humidity sensors per jar are a clean phase-2
    addition later: add a sensor reading, and instead of "mist because
    timer fired," mist because "timer fired AND humidity is below target,"
    using the same relay/interlock logic you already built.
